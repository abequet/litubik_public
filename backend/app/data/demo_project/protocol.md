# Smartphone use and pedestrian safety — example systematic review

*This is the sandbox example review of the LitUbik demo. The 60 references are
real (bibliographic metadata from OpenAlex, which is CC0); the screening
decisions are illustrative. Abstracts are deliberately not shipped — they stay
the publishers' copyright, whatever the licence of the metadata around them.
Run “Enrich abstracts” from the Snowballing page to fetch them at use time.*

## Background

Smartphone use while walking ("distracted walking") is a growing road-safety
concern. Observational and experimental studies report changes in crossing
behaviour, gait and situation awareness among phone-using pedestrians, but
the evidence is scattered across traffic-safety, ergonomics and public-health
venues.

## Research question

**In pedestrians (P), how does smartphone use while walking or crossing (E),
compared with undistracted walking (C), affect safety-related behaviour and
crash or near-miss risk (O)?**

## Eligibility criteria

### Inclusion
- **I1 — Population**: pedestrians of any age in real or simulated road
  environments.
- **I2 — Exposure**: interacting with a phone/smartphone (texting, calling,
  browsing, listening) while walking or crossing.
- **I3 — Outcomes**: safety-related behaviour (crossing decisions, gait,
  attention) or crash/near-miss/injury indicators.

### Exclusion
- **E1 — Wrong population**: drivers, cyclists or other non-pedestrian road
  users only.
- **E2 — Wrong exposure**: no phone interaction measured (generic
  distraction, headphones only, app design without walking task).
- **E3 — Wrong publication type**: reviews, editorials, conference abstracts
  without original data.

## Methods (summary)

Databases: OpenAlex (demo). Title/abstract screening in dual review with
conflict resolution, then full-text screening against the criteria above.
Data extraction covers design, setting (naturalistic / lab / VR), sample,
exposure type and outcomes.
